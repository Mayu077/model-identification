# Field Research: Questions to Ask Truck Owners

Before pitching this app to potential customers, you need real data. These questions will help you understand the actual pain points, willingness to pay, and objections you'll face.

**Target:** 5–10 truck owners near JNPT/Panvel, mix of 1-truck and small fleet (5-10 trucks).

**Duration:** 30–45 minutes per conversation (can do over chai/lunch).

---

## Section 1: Current Process & Pain Points

### The Tally Work

1. **Who does your billing today?** (You personally, a family member, an accountant, office staff?)
   - Follow-up: How many hours per day do they spend on this?
   - Follow-up: Do they make mistakes? How often? What happens when they do?

2. **Walk me through a trip entry right now.** (From trip card to bill — every step)
   - Follow-up: Where do you write the date, container #, weight? On paper first, or straight to the ledger/excel?
   - Follow-up: Do you double-check the container number against the port slip or just trust the driver's handwriting?

3. **What information do you need from a trip?** (Container no, pickup port, dropoff port, weight, date, customer name, rate paid?)
   - Follow-up: What's optional vs. what's a dealbreaker to get wrong?

4. **How much do you currently spend on tally work per month?** (Direct salary + benefits if it's staff, OR accountant fees if you outsource)
   - Follow-up: Would reducing that be valuable?

### Container & Port Issues

5. **Have you ever had a container number rejected by the port because it was entered wrong?** 
   - Follow-up: What happened? Did you have to re-file, pay a penalty, lose the trip from billing?
   - Follow-up: How often does this happen?

6. **What ports do you use?** (JNPT, GTI, Nhava Sheva, BMCT, others?)
   - Follow-up: Do your drivers sometimes forget which port they went to?
   - Follow-up: Do you have a list of which customer uses which port, or does the driver need to remember?

### Driver Behavior

7. **Do your drivers sometimes overstate trip dates to get bonuses or hide late arrivals?**
   - Follow-up: Do you audit trip cards against port slips to catch it?
   - Follow-up: How much of a problem is this really?

8. **How do you train a new driver to fill in the trip card?**
   - Follow-up: Do they still make mistakes on their first week?

9. **Can your drivers read and write in English?**
   - Follow-up: Would an app with Hindi/Marathi interface make a difference?

---

## Section 2: What They Need (Your App's Value Proposition)

### Speed & Accuracy

10. **If someone could extract trip data from a photo in 30 seconds (instead of typing it by hand), how valuable would that be?**
    - Scale: Not useful / Nice to have / Would save real time / Deal-changer
    - Follow-up: What's your threshold — how much time-saving makes it worth switching to something new?

11. **If the app caught container-number mistakes before they reached the bill, would that prevent the rejections you mentioned in Q5?**
    - Scale: Definitely / Probably / Maybe / No, wouldn't help
    - Follow-up: What else would need to be caught?

### Trust & Control

12. **If you could see an audit trail showing exactly which driver entered which trip, and when, would that help you trust the numbers more?**
    - Follow-up: Would that help your accountant or the tax authorities?

13. **Would you want the app to lock certain fields (like the date) so a driver can't change them after the fact?**
    - Follow-up: Which fields would you want locked vs. editable?

### Integration & Switching

14. **Do you use Excel, a tally book, or accounting software (Tally, QuickBooks, etc.) to track trips?**
    - Follow-up: Can you export from it? (This tells you how easy it would be to integrate later)

15. **If you had to pay a subscription for an app like this, what would make it feel worth it vs. not worth it?**
    - Follow-up: Would you want to pay per trip, per truck, or a flat monthly fee?
    - Follow-up: How much is the pain right now — would you pay ₹500/month to reduce it? ₹1000? ₹2000?

---

## Section 3: Objections & Concerns (Listen for these)

### The Accountant / CA's Perspective

16. **If you have an accountant or CA, what would they need to trust this app?**
    - Follow-up: Would they want a backup of all data? An audit log they can download?
    - Follow-up: Would they worry about data security? Where do they think the data should be stored?

### The "I Still Have to Check" Objection

17. **Even if the app fills in trip data, you'd still need to review it before billing, right?**
    - Follow-up: Would it be faster to review + correct a few mistakes than to type from scratch?
    - Stopwatch this: Have them pull an old trip card. Time how long it takes them to enter it manually. Then show them what an AI-filled entry looks like and time the review+correction. This single number is your pitch.

### Scale & Competition Concerns

18. **Would you ever consider using the app to bill on behalf of your customers** (if you grew into a fleet aggregator)?
    - Follow-up: Or would you only use it for your own trucks?
    - (This is early-stage thinking; don't worry if they say "no, just for us," that's fine.)

### Privacy & Data

19. **Are you worried about uploading trip cards (with driver names, vehicle #s) to the internet?**
    - Follow-up: If the data is encrypted and only you can see it, would that change your mind?
    - Follow-up: Would you want to be able to delete old data after a certain time (for privacy)?

---

## Section 4: Fleet Size & Pricing Anchor

20. **How many trucks do you operate?**

21. **How do you expect that to grow in the next 1–2 years?**
    - Follow-up: If you grew to 20 trucks, would you hire more office staff, or try to automate the billing?

22. **What do you currently spend on your tally operator (or accountant) per month for billing work?**
    - Follow-up: If an app cut that cost by 50%, would you switch?

23. **If the app cost ₹599/month for 1–5 trucks or ₹1,299/month for 5–10 trucks, would that be reasonable?**
    - Follow-up: What's your actual budget for software per year?

---

## Section 5: Willingness to Pilot

24. **Would you be willing to let me test this app on your business for a week or two, for free?**
    - Follow-up: If it works and saves you time, would you be open to paying for it after the trial?
    - Follow-up: What would need to happen in that trial for you to say "yes, I want this"?

25. **If you liked the app, would you recommend it to other truck owners you know?**
    - Follow-up: Who would you recommend it to? Can you introduce me?

---

## Section 6: Specific Feedback (If showing them the app)

26. **Can you walk me through entering a trip using this interface? Does it feel natural?**
    - Observe: Where do they click first? What confuses them?
    - Listen: Do they like the photo upload, or do they prefer manual entry?

27. **If a container number fails the checksum, does this error message make sense?** (Show them the red "Check digit failed" error)
    - Follow-up: Would you want more explanation, or is this enough?

28. **Would you want this app to send you a notification when a driver submits a trip?**
    - Follow-up: Or would you prefer to check it yourself without alerts?

## How to Record & Use Answers

### Keep It Simple
- Write notes by hand during the call (looks more respectful than typing on a laptop)
- After the call, spend 10 minutes typing up the key points

### Organize by Theme
Create a Google Doc or Notion page with columns:
| Owner Name | # Trucks | Current Tally Cost | Biggest Pain | Would Try App? | Price Sensitivity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Look for Patterns
After 5–10 calls, you should see:
- **Common pains:** (e.g., 8/10 owners mention container-number mistakes)
- **Willingness to pay:** (e.g., "nobody wants to pay more than ₹1000/month")
- **Deal-breakers:** (e.g., "if it doesn't integrate with my accountant, I won't use it")
- **Unexpected wins:** (e.g., "they love the audit trail more than I expected")

### Prioritize Your Next Build
Let the data tell you what matters:
- If 70% mention date-fraud concerns → prioritize the port-slip timestamp check
- If 60% say "yes, I'd pay ₹1000/month" → validate that's your price point
- If nobody mentions Hindi/Marathi → don't build it yet

---

## Red Flags to Listen For

- **"I'll wait and see what others do first"** → They're risk-averse; you need a free trial + case study from an early adopter
- **"My accountant would never trust this"** → You need an accountant co-sign or at least a detailed audit-log export
- **"We're happy with how things are"** → They're genuinely not in pain (move to the next owner)
- **"How much does it cost?" (asked first, before asking what it does)** → Price-sensitive; anchor low
- **"This is interesting but I'd need to ask my partner/accountant/CA"** → They're a decision-maker but not the only one; get the other stakeholder in the room

---

## After the Calls: Your Playbook

Once you've completed 5–10 interviews:

1. **Update your pitch** based on what actually resonates (not what you assumed)
2. **Set your price** based on what they said they'd pay, not your guess
3. **Build the next feature** based on their top 3 pain points, not your roadmap
4. **Find your first pilot customer** — someone who said "yes, I'd try it" and whose pain is in the top 3 across all interviews
5. **Measure** — timing, accuracy, mistakes caught — before you scale

Good luck. The answers you get here are worth more than any investor advice.
